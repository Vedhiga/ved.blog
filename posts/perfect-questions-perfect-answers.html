Perfect Questions, Perfect Answers

A book that answered all my questions

Draft — May 2025 · Personal reflection · by Dhruv DRAFT

I came to this book by accident. I was looking for something on Vedic philosophy that wasn't either too academic or too devotional-pamphlet. A friend mentioned it. I picked it up expecting an hour's read. I've gone back to it many times since.

Perfect Questions, Perfect Answers is a record of real conversations between Srila Prabhupada and Bob Cohen — a young American Peace Corps volunteer in India in 1972. Bob asks everything. Prabhupada answers everything. It's 70 pages and it's one of the most honest spiritual dialogues I've ever read.

Why Bob's Questions Work

Bob Cohen doesn't perform humility. He genuinely doesn't know. He asks things like: "What is the best thing I can do for the people around me?" and "Why do bad things happen to good people?" and "Is God in everything?" — not as debate points but as actual questions he's carrying.

That's the "perfect" in the title. Not that the questions are philosophically sophisticated — they're not. They're perfect because they're sincere. And Prabhupada, who spent a lifetime answering insincere questions from proud people, responds to Bob's sincerity with unusual warmth and patience.

Exchanges That Stayed With Me

What is the best way I can help people?
You cannot help people. Only Krishna can help people. You can simply be an instrument. Don't be proud that you are helping. That will bind you.
What is the meaning of "pure"? A pure devotee?
One who has no material desire. One acts only for Krishna — not for himself, not for his family, not for his nation. Only for the Supreme.

These aren't the answers I expected. I expected philosophy. Instead I got something more like a direct correction of how I was thinking about service, ego, and purpose.

My Reflection

What struck me most is that Prabhupada never condescends to Bob. He treats a 20-something American volunteer's genuine confusion with the same seriousness as a scholar's inquiry. That itself taught me something about what a teacher looks like. — My note while reading, March 2025

I'm a student. I ask a lot of questions — about math, physics, code, about what I'm supposed to be doing with my life. This book showed me that the quality of your question depends entirely on whether you actually want an answer, or whether you're performing curiosity to seem interesting.

I've been trying to ask better questions since. More Bob Cohen, less debate-club.

Where to Read It

The full text is available free at vedabase.io — the official Bhaktivedanta archives. It's a short read. I'd suggest reading it in one sitting if you can.
